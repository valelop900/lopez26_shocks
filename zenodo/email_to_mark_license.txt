Hi Mark,

One thing I can't decide on my own for the Zenodo release: the license.

I've put CC-BY-4.0 in as a placeholder, since that's what most astronomy data deposits use
and it's the least restrictive thing that still requires attribution. But I don't know
whether NRAO has a standing preference for data products that come out of the student
program, or whether this needs to go past anyone there before we publish. I'd rather ask
than assume. If CC-BY-4.0 is fine, I'll swap the placeholder for the real license text and
that part is done.

Separately, I checked the paper's figures and numbers against the release tables, and two
things came up that I don't think I should decide on my own.

The first is the jet power. The Discussion says we converted 3 GHz to 151 MHz assuming a
spectral index of -0.7, and that's genuinely what plot_loi_vs_q.py does, so Figure 4 is the
flat version. But jet_power.py uses each source's own Alpha and only falls back to -0.7
where Alpha is blank, and the medians we quote in that same sentence, 35.5 and 35.6, are the
per-source ones. Flat -0.7 gives 35.11 for the small sample and 35.76 for the large one.
That's a factor of about 4.5 between them, which I don't think supports the "nearly
identical median jet powers" line in the Conclusions. So the sentence and the figure agree
with each other but not with the two numbers in the sentence. Which one did you intend?

The second is about the tables rather than the paper. The BPT ratios we plot in Figure 1
come from our own pyspeckit fits, but the tables ship the agngal fluxes instead. If someone
downloads the release and recomputes log([OI]/Ha) and log([OIII]/Hb) from the flux columns,
they get something different by about 0.15 dex typically and by up to a dex in the worst
cases, and they can't reproduce sl_distance or redraw Figure 1. Same for the [OI]
luminosity in Figure 4, which uses the fitted flux, not the agngal one. The fix is easy - I
can add log_oi_ha and log_oiii_hb as columns, which makes sl_distance reproducible - and
I've set it up so it's a one-line switch. I'd like to turn it on unless you object.

Everything else I checked does reproduce: the BPT counts, the W80 box plot down to the
whiskers and outliers, the SL histogram, both Mann-Whitney and KS p-values, the [Ne V]
fractions including the matched-redshift and Halpha/Hbeta < 4 versions, and the type-1
counts.

One smaller thing: the emission line fluxes come from the agngal VAC. I've written the unit
as 1e-17 erg/s/cm2 in the column descriptions but I haven't confirmed that's right - do you
know offhand? And your August note said to check the Willott 1999 normalization on ADS; the
formula printed in the paper matches what jet_power.py does, including f = 1, so I've taken
that as settled unless you say otherwise.

The four bigger decisions from my last email are still open too, but the license is the one
actually holding things up, since I can't publish without it.
