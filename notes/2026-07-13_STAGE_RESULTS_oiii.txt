THE OBJECTS
I looked at pictures of every object and kept the good ones.

Good = the radio blob sits on a galaxy.
After checking: 16 in the large sample (radio bigger than 20 kpc),
60 in the small sample (radio 10-15 kpc).

2 objects had to be thrown out when I tried to fit them:
  - one has a dead spot in its spectrum right where the OIII lne should be,
    so there is nothing to measure (missing data).
  - one just would not fit at all.


HOW I FIT THE LINE
For each object I fit the OIII line with two bell curves added together:
  - a narrow one (calm gas in the galaxy)
  - a wide one (fast gas being pushed out, the outflow)
I keep their widths in separate ranges so they can't swap jobs.
I also fit the smaller partner line (4959)

FIRST RESULT: THE "COUNT" - and why it is NOT real
I counted how many objects have a real wide part.
Rule: the wide part is real if it is at least 3 times bigger than its error.
  - large sample: most objects have a wide part
  - small sample: fewer objects have a wide part
At first this looks like large sources have more outflows. But it is fake.
The reason: the rule depends on how bright and clean the spectrum is.
Bright lines pass the rule easily. Faint lines don't, even if the wide
part is really there. My large sample lines are about twice as bright,
so of course they pass more often.
I proved this two ways:
  - if I only compare the bright objects, both samples pass 100% of the time,
    no difference at all.
  - if I add noise to the large sample to make it as faint as the small one,
    its pass rate drops down to match the small sample.
So the count was measuring brightness, not outflows.
(Same mistake I already caught in the OI line, which is much fainter.)

SECOND RESULT: THE WIDTH - this is the real one
Instead of counting yes/no, I measured how WIDE each line is.
Width is a real number, so it does not have the brightness problem.
I measured width three different ways on purpose. All three agree:
the small sample is wider (more disturbed gas).
  - my own combined width:        large 443   small 543
  - a width from the whole line
    shape (my most trusted one):  large 548   small 708
  - the survey's own catalog width
    (not my code at all):         large 214   small 227
The last one is smaller and closer because it uses a simpler fit that
misses the faint edges of the line, but it still leans the same way.
All numbers are in km/s (a wider line means faster, more disturbed gas).

A THING I CHECKED AND CLEARED
A few objects got fit with their whole line put into the wide slot and
nothing in the narrow slot. I worried this was faking the width result.
I checked by eye: these are just single wide lines, not a mistake.
When I remove them, the small sample is still wider. And my most trusted
width measurement doesn't use the two slots at all, so it can't be fooled
by this anyway. So this is not the cause of the result.


WHAT THIS MEANS
Small radio sources have wider OIII lines than large ones, meaning more
disturbed gas. It shows up in every way I measured it.
